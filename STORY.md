# Honaru: Story

This file owns what the landing page says and how it should feel.
DESIGN.md owns how it looks (colors, type, layout, motion tokens).
Read both before touching the landing page. If they conflict on visuals, DESIGN.md wins. If they conflict on words or tone, this file wins.

---

## The line

**Hero:** Open less and love more.
**Sub:** Six apps for one relationship is five too many.

The product argues for using itself less. Honaru exists so the apps stop getting in the way of the people.

---

## The feeling

The visitor should go from **recognition** ("ugh, yes, that's me") to **relief** ("oh, it could be one place") to **warmth** ("this is for my people") to **permission** ("okay, now I can put my phone down").

The last feeling matters most. The page should end by letting people leave.

---

## The story (StoryBrand)

- **Hero:** someone who loves people across a few circles: a partner, family, friends, trips.
- **Problem, external:** one app to message, one for photos and stories, one for the calendar, one for splitting the bill, one for where they are, one for little shared widgets.
- **Problem, internal:** the switching is tiring, and the people you love start to feel like tabs.
- **Problem, philosophical:** the people you love shouldn't be scattered across six places.
- **Stakes:** closeness rarely ends all at once. It slips through the gaps between apps.
- **Guide:** Honaru, one home for everything you share with your people.
  - Empathy: the founder moment (see placeholder below).
  - Authority: four Spaces (Couple, Family, Friends, Travel), each built for how that group actually shows up.
- **Plan:** pick a Space, bring your people in, let the other five go.
- **Success:** you open fewer apps and spend the time on the people.

## Voice

- Warm, dry, short sentences. A little wry. Never preachy.
- Lead with the feeling, then the thing.
- The product tells people to use it less. Lean into that.
- Never guilt people for being far away or busy.
- "Six" is a rhetorical count (messaging, photos and stories, calendar, expenses, location, widgets). Use it consistently, never a different number.

**Avoid (these make love sound transactional):** manage, admin, handle, logistics, coordinate, organize, optimize, productivity, streamline, seamless, platform, solution, all-in-one.

**Say instead:** share, keep, bring in, find each other, remember, be there.

## What the agent should build (checkable)

Feelings are not instructions. These are.

1. **Restraint.** One idea per viewport. No carousels, pop-ups, cookie-style banners, or sticky nags.
2. **One CTA per viewport.** Same label everywhere.
3. **Hero is five words or fewer** and sits alone with its sub line. Nothing else competes with it.
4. **Motion is slow and exhale-like** (ease-out, roughly 600-900ms). Nothing bounces. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` with a static version.
5. **The page ends.** The final viewport has no nav and no link-heavy footer, only the closing line and one button. Legal links may sit small beneath.
6. **No banned words.** Search the copy for the avoid list above before finishing.
7. **No invented content.** Leave the founder moment and CTA label as visible placeholders until filled. No fake testimonials, user counts, or logos.
8. **Visuals come from DESIGN.md.** Do not introduce new colors, fonts, or spacing here.

When done, the agent should state which of the eight checks pass and flag anything it could not verify.

## Summary

Background for agents, not a build spec: use it to understand what Honaru is and how the landing page's body text should sound.

- **What it is:** one home for everything you share with your people, in place of the six apps a relationship ends up spread across (messaging, photos and stories, calendar, the bill, location, widgets).
- **Who it's for:** people who love others across a few circles: a partner, family, friends, the people they travel with.
- **The story:** switching apps is tiring, and the people you love start to feel like tabs. Closeness rarely ends all at once; it slips through the gaps. Honaru brings it back to one place, so you open less and spend the time on them.
- **What the copy should do:** move the reader from recognition to relief to warmth, and finally to permission to put the phone down. Warm, dry, short sentences; lead with the feeling, never guilt anyone, and never make love sound transactional.
